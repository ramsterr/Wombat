What is a Nash Equilibrium?

A Nash Equilibrium is a set of strategies (one per player) where no player can improve their payoff by unilaterally changing their strategy, given what the others do. Each player is already playing a best response to the others.

In the Prisoner's Dilemma above, payoffs follow T > R > P > S (temptation > reward > punishment > sucker). Defect is a dominant strategy for both, so the unique NE is (Defect, Defect) — even though mutual cooperation (R,R) is Pareto-better. This gap is the dilemma.

Mixed strategies: when no pure NE exists, players randomize. The NE occurs where each player's strategy makes the other indifferent (best-response curves cross). The green dot on the diagram marks the equilibrium probability of cooperation.

Key facts: Nash proved every finite game has at least one NE (possibly in mixed strategies). NE need not be socially optimal, unique, or fair — it is simply stable against unilateral deviation.
